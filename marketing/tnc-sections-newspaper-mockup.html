This week's mover

Cohiba Behike 52. Marseille undercut Copenhagen by €18.

The five-week window closed today. Le Havane in the 6ème pushed 40 boxes into the market at a €648 price point — €18.20 under the Copenhagen average, €41 under the Zürich equivalent, and the lowest per-cigar spend on a Behike in the European Union this year.

What moved it: a shipment cleared French customs three weeks after the second-quarter Habanos SA release, at a favourable EUR/USD spread. The next major shipment isn't expected until August.

Reportage · Words by Cristian Ortiz Suárez · Photographs by Jonas Bendiksen

Inside Casa del Habano, Copenhagen.

An afternoon on Strøget with the 84-year-old torcedor who rolls Behikes on a bench older than most of the guests — and what the rest of Europe can learn from a shop that still sells cigars the way they were sold in Havana in 1962.

On the second floor of a building on Strøget that used to be a hat shop, a man named Ricardo Padrón unrolls a bundle of Vuelta Abajo filler and begins the process of turning it into a cigar. He is 84 years old. He has done this every working day since 1962. The bench in front of him — three metres of Spanish cedar, warped in the middle from six decades of pressure — is older than every person in the room including the cat sleeping in the wing chair by the window.

The room is warm. It smells like a humidor that has never been closed. Padrón takes a Behike-size filler bundle in his left hand, holds it under his chin to measure it, and reaches for the binder leaf.
