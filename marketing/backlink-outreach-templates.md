# Backlink outreach — templates + target list

Three templated emails for the three audiences that will move the SEO needle fastest. Send 5–10 of each per week. Reply rate at this volume is typically 15–25%; backlink rate from replies is 30–50%. So expect ~1–3 placed links per 10 sends.

The principles behind the templates:
1. **Lead with value, not the ask.** Each opening sentence has to be useful to the recipient even if they never click your link.
2. **Specific, not generic.** Mention something from their last piece. Cuban-cigar editors hate cold mass blasts.
3. **Easy yes.** Make the linkable thing exist already (the Finder, a blog post). Don't ask them to write something new.
4. **No PR-speak.** "Killer tool" / "industry-leading" / "exciting launch" all earn instant trash. Plain language wins.
5. **One ask per email.** Either a link, or a quote, or a feature mention. Never all three.

Send from `guatabeycigars@gmail.com` today. If you set up `cris@thenextcigar.com` via Cloudflare Email Routing (see `marketing/setup-custom-email-cloudflare.md`), reply rate roughly triples vs gmail. Plain text, no signature graphics, no tracking pixels.

No expectation of a link in return. The tool exists because I got tired of doing the comparison by hand and figured other people would want it too.

**Why this works:** Opens with proof you read their work, offers genuine value (a free fact-check service), states the project facts concisely, explicitly says "no expectation of a link" — which is exactly what makes editors more likely to give one. The link is mentioned once, in passing, embedded in the value pitch.

## Reply handling

If they say **yes** to a mention/link:
- Send them the latest snapshot of whatever data they asked about within 24h.
- Don't ask "when will it run" — that kills the relationship.
- After their piece runs, send a thank-you note. One paragraph. No follow-up ask.

If they say **maybe** / "send me more info":
- Send a single follow-up with a specific data example: "Here's the May 2026 Cohiba Robusto ranking across 5 retailers, in case it's useful for the piece."
- Don't follow up again unless they engage. 1 maybe + 1 follow-up + silence = move on.

If they say **no** or don't respond:
- Don't follow up. Move on. Try again in 3 months on a different piece.
- Editors who said no on round 1 sometimes say yes on round 3 once your site has more traction.

If they **ask for payment** / sponsored placement:
- Decline politely. The Finder's credibility depends on no paid placements.
- "Appreciate the offer — we're keeping it editorial-only at this stage. If that changes I'll be in touch."

## Metrics to track

In a simple spreadsheet, log:

| Date sent | Target | Template | Response | Link placed? | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Review monthly. After 30 sends:
- Reply rate < 10% → templates aren't landing. Rewrite.
- Reply rate > 25%, link rate < 30% → asks are unclear or too big. Lower the bar.
- Reply rate > 25%, link rate > 40% → keep going, accelerate volume.

After 90 days expect: ~5–10 placed backlinks from quality EU cigar sources. Each one is worth roughly 50–200 referral visits over the following year and a meaningful domain-authority bump for the Finder.

## Don't do this

- Bulk send via Mailchimp or similar. Editors detect mail-merge in two sentences.
- Use a fake personal address. Send from your own real address.
- Promise reciprocity ("if you link me I'll link you"). Sounds slimy and editors hate it.
- Send to general info@ addresses. Find the specific person (LinkedIn, masthead, About page).
- Pitch before the site has real traffic / depth. We're past that bar — 60+ SKU pages, 6+ blog posts, working scraper, real data. Send away.
