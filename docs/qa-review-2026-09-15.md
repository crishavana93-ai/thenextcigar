# thenextcigar.com — QA and usability review

**15 September 2026.** Method: scripted browser sessions (Chromium, iPhone 13 viewport 390×844 with touch emulation, then 1440×900 desktop), private context, against the live site. Every finding below was reproduced at least twice; screenshots are in the session. Times are wall-clock from the first tap.

**Not done, and why.** Task 5 (join the Lounge and add a shop) was not executed: I don't create accounts on someone's behalf. The signup form was tested to the point of submission only. Task 6 was completed on desktop with the paper's own inbox (subscriptions@thenextcigar.com); the confirmation email and its unsubscribe link should be in that mailbox now — that last step is yours to check.

## Verdict

The site does the hard thing well: a stranger on desktop knows in three seconds that this is a newspaper about Cuban cigars with a live price board, and the board, the landed-cost estimate and the box-code decoder all work and say what they can't know. The money paths are where it fails. On a phone, the Finder, every vitola page, every country page and the shop index rendered at roughly 40% scale because one line of small print was allowed to be 841px wide — every tap target on your second-biggest page was a quarter of its intended size, and the "Save price alert" button could not be tapped at all. That is fixed in commit `92532ca`, pending your push. Behind it sit three trust problems on the exact page a buyer lands on: the shop's flagship cutter page makes claims that contradict each other and the AliExpress cart you are about to check out; the vitola page headlines a four-month-old price as "cheapest in stock" with a Buy button that lands on a category page; and the privacy policy describes a site that no longer exists. None of these is expensive to fix. All of them are cheaper than the sale they cost.

## Three things that impressed

The box-code decoder is the most honest tool of its kind I have tested: it decodes what is decodable, refuses the factory letters with a reason, and says why other sites' answers are guesses. The vitola page's landed-cost column is the feature nobody else has and it worked first time, with a sentence that tells you what it is and isn't. And the whole site is fast and clean: 1.6 s to load on a throttled mobile profile, zero JavaScript errors on eleven pages, and it degrades to readable static HTML with scripts off.

## Five fixes to ship first

1. **Push `92532ca`.** Until it deploys, every phone reader of the Finder is looking at the page at 40% and cannot tap the alert button; this is the single largest usability defect on the site and it is one CSS rule.
2. **Rewrite the four-in-one cutter page to say only what you know**, as was done for the other four products: delete the EU-warehouse line, the "same supplier family" line and the brass/60-ring specs, state the origin and the import-charge possibility plainly, and replace the CDN image with your own photograph the day the parcel lands. It is the product your best-performing article points to.
3. **Never headline a stale price.** "Cheapest in stock" and the Buy button should consider live rows only; a May row can appear in the table with its date, but not as the answer. The same rule was applied to the "mover" yesterday and belongs here too.
4. **Relabel non-product links.** When a retailer's URL is a category or collection, the button reads "Browse at …", not "Buy →". One conditional in the row template.
5. **Rewrite the privacy policy to match the site and add "Delete my account" to the profile page**, because you now hold accounts, orders, alerts and a humidor ledger under a policy that says you hold an email address, and GDPR gives every member the right to leave with their data.

Then, when there is time: a search box in the masthead that covers vitolas, retailers and articles; a single line above the fold on mobile saying what the paper is and linking to the board; and prices shown in the reader's currency with USD as the settlement note, not the headline.
