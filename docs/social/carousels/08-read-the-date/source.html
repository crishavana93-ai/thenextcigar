The date, since 2000 · three letters, two digits
ABR 24April 2024. The first three letters of the Spanish month, then the last two digits of the year. That is the whole system, unchanged since January 2000.
How to read it

Turn the box over.

Under the marks burnt into the cedar, two crooked lines of blue or black ink. A hand stamp, a few hundred boxes a day. Too perfect worries us more than a little crooked.

What the date means · filled, not harvested
+2 yearsThe stamp is the month the box was filled. The leaf inside was fermented and aged for a year and a half to two years before anyone rolled it. A 2024 box holds 2022 tobacco.
Our rule

Not in its first year.

Fresh Habanos often smoke sharp and closed for months after boxing. From about eighteen months the box settles. Same cigar, same price, two dates on the shelf: take the older one.

2000–ENE 00  Plain Spanish month from January 2000, and ever since.
Vintage boxes

N I V E L A C U S O

N is 1, I is 2, V is 3 … O is 0. Month first, in one or two letters, then the year in two. Our decoder reads it for you.

The factory code · the second line
2003The year Habanos S.A. started rotating the three-letter factory code on a schedule it keeps to itself. Enough combinations that a code need not repeat for years. The Cuban Cigar Website calls tracking them “virtually impossible”.
The myth

Nobody can decode it.

The confident tables online — EL for El Laguito, FPG for Partagás — are the 1985–1998 list under a new heading. For any modern box, the factory line tells you nothing. We say so, rather than guess.

Does the stamp prove it's real?
No.Forgers copy stamps off real boxes. A date you recognise proves nothing; a code you don't recognise condemns nothing. What a bad stamp can do is confirm a doubt: ENE 98 is impossible, printed-flat marks are a tell, and a price far under the board is the tell nobody can fake.
