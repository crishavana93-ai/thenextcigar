Humidor dropping since the heating came on?

It's probably not broken. Heated Swedish homes often get very dry in winter. Why it happens, and the fixes →

The Next Cigar · photo: Jojoo64, CC BY-SA 4.01 / 7
The problem · winter air indoors
<20%Most readings in the driest group of homes were below 20% humidity. The study: 678 Swedish homes, heating season 2007–08. About 37% fell in the two driest groups.
The cause

Dry room, thirsty box.

A wooden humidor isn't sealed tight, so it trades moisture with the room around it. When the room is dry, the box loses moisture.

The Next Cigar · source: REHVA Journal 6/2021 · photo: Maria Eklind, CC BY-SA 2.02 / 7
Where it leaks · the box itself
2–5 pointsHow far below its humidity pack's level a porous wooden humidor can sit, in percentage points, says Boveda. Leaky seals drain it too, and Boveda calls glass tops "drafty".
Why

Wood breathes, seals wear.

Nothing has to be broken. The box is simply losing moisture faster than the pack gives it back.

The Next Cigar · source: Boveda · photo: MajestyWoodworks, CC BY 4.03 / 7
Step 1 · check the gauge first
6 monthsBoveda's advice: recalibrate a hygrometer every six months. If yours reads low, test it before you change anything else.
Check first

Is it the box, or the gauge?

The salt test: damp table salt sealed in a bag holds the air at 75%. Our post from 11 November shows it step by step.

Step 2 · one step up for winter
69Want 69%? Use a 72% pack.
72Want 72%? Use a 75% pack.
75Glass-top humidor? Go to 75%.
Fix it

Raise the pack one level.

Boveda's own advice for a humidor that runs low: a pack one step higher than the humidity you want.

The Next Cigar · source: Boveda · photo: T.Voekler, CC BY-SA 3.05 / 7
Steps 3 and 4 · if it still won't hold
03Worn or leaky seal? Double the number of packs.
04Very dry room? Move to something airtight: a sealed humidor bag, a tupperdor (a sealed plastic box) or an acrylic humidor.
