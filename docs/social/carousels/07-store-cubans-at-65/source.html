The rule

Habanos publishes 65–70%. Our storage guide explains why we keep Cuban cigars at the low end of it.

Why it matters

Too wet is as bad as too dry.

Too much moisture swells the filler and can split the wrapper; too little leaves it brittle. Staying inside Habanos' range avoids both.

Temperature · the part people skip
18°CHabanos' ceiling. Its reason is the tobacco beetle, which breeds in warmth. A warm humidor is an incubator.
Temperature

Fix the heat first, the humidity follows.

Humidity moves with temperature, so Habanos says to keep it steady. Use the coolest room in the flat, away from the radiator and the window.

Holding 65% in a Swedish winter · five rules
01Two-way packs at 65, not sponges. Add a pack if the reading drops in winter.
02Season a new humidor before a single cigar goes in.
03Keep the dress boxes closed inside the humidor. Two layers of cedar, two buffers.
04Open the lid less. Every opening lets dry winter air in.
05A calibrated hygrometer inside, where the cigars are.
The method

Heating on, desert air.

Heated flats in a northern winter are very dry, and a wooden humidor loses moisture through its walls.

Calibrate the gauge · the salt test
75%Damp table salt, sealed in a bag with the hygrometer for 8–24 hours. It should read 75. The difference is your offset; check again every six months.
Check it

Your gauge can be wrong.

Boveda says even a calibrated hygrometer is accurate to about ±3–5%. Measure inside the box, where the cigars are.
