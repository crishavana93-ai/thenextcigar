Straight cutter

One or two blades remove part of the cap in one motion.

Cut at the shoulder, where the curved head starts to straighten, and leave some of the cap glued in place.

The Next Cigar · source: Cigar Aficionado, “How to cut a cigar”2 / 12
02 / 10

V-cutter

Cuts a wedge-shaped notch across the cap instead of slicing it off.

It removes less of the cap than a straight cut, so more of the cap stays in place at the head.

The Next Cigar · source: our explanation of the tool3 / 12
03 / 10

Punch cutter

A round blade bores a hole in the centre of the cap.

The hole is a fixed size, so on a thin cigar it opens a larger share of the head than on a thick one.

The Next Cigar · source: our explanation of the tool4 / 12
04 / 10

Torch lighter

Burns butane in a pressurised jet that wind does not easily blow out.

Butane boils at about −0.5 °C. As it gets colder, the pressure in the tank drops, which is why torches weaken in the cold.

The Next Cigar · source: NIST Chemistry WebBook, butane5 / 12
05 / 10

Soft-flame lighter

Burns butane in an open flame, like a match.

Cigar Aficionado prefers butane. With an oil lighter, it says to let the flame burn for a moment first.

The Next Cigar · source: Cigar Aficionado, “How to light a cigar”6 / 12
06 / 10

Cedar spill

A thin strip of cedar, lit from a match and used as a longer flame.

With ordinary matches, Cigar Aficionado says to let the sulfur burn off before lighting.

The Next Cigar · source: Cigar Aficionado, “How to light a cigar”7 / 12
07 / 10

The humidor

A closed box that keeps humidity steady. Keep it where the temperature stays stable.

Published industry storage guidance: 65–70% relative humidity at 16–18 °C.

The Next Cigar · source: industry storage guidance8 / 12
08 / 10

Hygrometer

Measures the relative humidity inside the box.

Check it with the salt test: a damp salt slurry in a sealed bag holds the air at about 75% at room temperature. The gap between the reading and 75% is its error.

The Next Cigar · source: Greenspan (1977), J. Res. NBS9 / 12
09 / 10

Two-way humidity pack

A pouch of salt and water behind a membrane that releases or absorbs moisture.

Packs are made for set levels, such as 65, 69, 72 and 75% relative humidity, and are replaced rather than refilled.

The Next Cigar · source: Cigar Aficionado, on Boveda packs10 / 12
10 / 10

Travel case

Rigid tubes or a sealed box for carrying cigars away from home.

It stops cigars being crushed, and a sealed case slows drying on short trips.
