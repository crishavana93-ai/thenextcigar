Part three. The court case that split the names. September 15, 1960. Cuba takes over five cigar companies, and puts in state managers, called interventors. The owners walk out of their factories for the last time. But the cigars keep going to the US, under the same names. So who owns those names now? And who gets the money? Three American importers end up in court, caught between the exiles and Cuba. In 1972, a federal judge in New York rules. Money for cigars shipped before the takeover goes to the exiled owners. Money for cigars shipped after goes to Cuba's interventors. And the names? In the US, Cuba's takeover didn't take them from the exiled owners. The twist: the owners win no damages. The court found they couldn't show any loss. Then the money fight reaches the Supreme Court. In 1976, five votes to four: refusing to repay an importer's mistaken payments was not an act of state. So the importer could collect. Cuba kept the factories. But in the US, the takeover never reached the names. Next: the name that was never seized. Link in bio.
