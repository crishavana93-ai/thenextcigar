## Caption (paste)

Twin Names, part 4: the name that was never seized.

• 1960: Cuba takes over its leading cigar makers. But Cohiba was never taken. Cuba made it.
• Fidel Castro told Cigar Aficionado in 1994 that a bodyguard knew a man who made cigars he liked. "We set up the house." The house became El Laguito, in Havana.
• Who rolled the first one? Cigar Aficionado has printed two versions: Avelino Lara, 1968 (1992 article), or Eduardo Rivera, early 1960s, with the name first used in 1966 (1995 article).
• For years Cohibas were best known as official gifts for heads of state and diplomats, and were sold only inside Cuba. The world launch came in 1982.
• December 1977: notes on General Cigar's internal memos call it "Castro's brand cigar". General applies for the name in the US in March 1978; the registration follows in 1981. In November 1992, as the cigar boom begins, General relaunches its own Cohiba.
• 1997: Cuba's state cigar company fights back, first at the US trademark board, then in a New York court. A New York judge sides with Cuba in 2004. In 2005 an appeals court rules the embargo blocks Cuba's claim, and the US Supreme Court declines to hear it.
• Back at the trademark board, Cuba's case rests on a 1929 treaty, the Pan American Convention. In 2014 a federal appeals court lets that claim go ahead. In 2022 the board orders the US registrations cancelled. In 2025 a federal judge in Virginia reviews the case afresh and reaches the same result.
• The twist: Cuban Cohibas still can't be sold in the US, and the American Cohiba is still on sale.
• 16 September 2026: three judges of the US 4th Circuit hear the appeal. As of 21 November 2026, the ruling is pending.
