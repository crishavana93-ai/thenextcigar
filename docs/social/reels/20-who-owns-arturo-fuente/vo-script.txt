Fire after fire. Four generations. Still the same family. Who owns Arturo Fuente?
In 1912, a twenty-four-year-old Cuban immigrant, Arturo Fuente, opened a cigar factory in West Tampa, Florida. By the early 1920s it had about five hundred workers.
In 1924 the factory burned while he was in Cuba buying tobacco. It put the company out of business for twenty-two years.
In the 1950s his son Carlos took over the business, for one dollar.
In the 1970s production moved to Nicaragua. That factory burned during the revolution.
In 1980 the family started again, in Santiago, in the Dominican Republic.
The industry said quality wrapper leaf couldn't be grown there. In 1991 the family planted it anyway, near Bonao.
Today, the company says, it is still family-owned. It is run by Carlos Fuente Junior. Since 1986 it has worked with another family firm, J.C. Newman. In 2001 the two families set up a charity that runs a school and a health centre in the Dominican Republic.
Who owns it? According to the company, the same family, one hundred and fourteen years later.
