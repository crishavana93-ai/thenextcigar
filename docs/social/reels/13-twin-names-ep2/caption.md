## Caption (paste, no-brands version)

Twin Names, part 2: the families who left.

• September 1960: soldiers enter Ramón Cifuentes' factory in Havana. "They asked me to stay," he told Cigar Aficionado in 1991. "I said no." He left for the US days later and went to work for the head of a big American tobacco company. After ten years of offers, his family made a deal in 1975. In 1977 one of his old names came back, made in Jamaica.
• In 1935 Alonso Menéndez created a name in Havana. A year later Pepe García joined him. In exile they started again in the Canary Islands, with a spin-off of that name.
• Fernando Palicio ended up in Florida. At a meeting in Hialeah he made a deal on two of his names. In all, he sold his names for almost $700,000.
• In 1968 the Trinidad family found a factory in Tampa to make their cigars. In 2001 they won the US trademark case over their name.

Four stories, four second lives. Every name, owner by owner, is on our site: link in bio.

Next: the court case that split the names. History, not advice. We don't sell tobacco.

Source: Gregory Mottola, "What's in a Brand Name?", Cigar Aficionado (2018).
