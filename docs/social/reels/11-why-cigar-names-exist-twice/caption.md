## Caption (paste)

Why do some famous cigar names exist twice? One made in Cuba, one made somewhere else.

• From 1960, Cuba's new government took over the cigar factories. Many owners left and started again abroad: the Canary Islands, the Dominican Republic, Honduras, Jamaica, Florida.
• In 1962 the US embargo banned imports of Cuban goods. In 1972 a US court ruled that several exiled owners still owned their brand names in the US, so non-Cuban versions could be sold there.
• Cigar Aficionado profiles ten famous names that exist in both a Cuban and a non-Cuban version.

What makes the Cuban one Cuban: the protected name Habano (a Protected Denomination of Origin), tobacco grown in Cuba, mainly in Vuelta Abajo, boxes stamped "Totalmente a mano" (totally by hand), and a warranty seal first introduced in 1889.

And the rest? Nicaragua, the Dominican Republic and Honduras made more than 99% of the handmade cigars the US imported in January–September 2025.

Different soil, different law, different history. Which fact surprised you?
