His Miami cigar factory was bombed in 1979, and again in 1980. Who owns Padrón?
José Orlando Padrón was born in Cuba in 1926. He left in 1961, and reached Miami in 1962, living on sixty dollars a month in refugee aid.
According to the company, a friend gave him a small hammer. He gardened by day, did carpentry by night, and saved six hundred dollars.
In September 1964 he opened a cigar company in Miami, with one roller.
He also opened a factory in Estelí, in Nicaragua. In May 1978 his factory and warehouse there burned.
That autumn he went to Havana with a group of Cuban exiles, for talks with Castro's government about political prisoners.
In 1979 and 1980, Omega 7, a militant anti-Castro exile group, claimed bombings of his Miami factory. Reports at the time, in the Miami Herald and New York magazine, noted his part in the Havana talks.
In 1985 a US embargo banned all imports from Nicaragua, and for a time in the 1980s the company rolled its cigars in Honduras.
He died in 2017, aged ninety-one. Today the company is still run by his family, led by his son Jorge.
One hammer. Three countries. Still the same family.
