## Caption (paste)

His Miami cigar factory was bombed in 1979, and again in 1980. This is how it started: with a small hammer.

• José Orlando Padrón left Havana in 1961 and reached Miami in 1962, getting $60 a month in refugee aid at first.
• According to the company, a friend gave him a small hammer. He gardened by day, did carpentry by night, and saved $600.
• September 1964: he opened a cigar company in Miami, with one roller.
• 1978: he travelled to Havana with a group of Cuban exiles for talks with Castro's government about freeing political prisoners.
• 1979 and 1980: Omega 7, a militant anti-Castro exile group, claimed bombings of his factory. Reports at the time noted his part in the Havana talks. The company blames "a small group of local extortionists".
• He died in 2017, aged 91. His son Jorge runs the company today.
