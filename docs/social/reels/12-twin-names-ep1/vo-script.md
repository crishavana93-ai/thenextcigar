Why do some famous cigar names exist twice? In 1960, Cuba's new government seized the five leading cigar makers. Most of their owners fled. Alonso Menéndez and Pepe García started again in the Canary Islands. Fernando Palicio reached the US and sold his names to a Tampa company. By 1965, the first of them was being made in Honduras. Then, in 1972, a New York court confirmed it: several exiled owners still owned their names in the US. Later, Ramón Cifuentes oversaw one of his old names being made again, in Jamaica. So today the same names come from two places. Cuba, and the Dominican Republic, Honduras or Nicaragua. One twist. One name was created in Cuba in 1966. A US company registered it there anyway.
