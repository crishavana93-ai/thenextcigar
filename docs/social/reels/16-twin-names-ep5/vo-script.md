Part five. Who owns the twin names today?

Start in Havana. Every Cuban Cohiba, Montecristo and Partagás is sold worldwide through one company, Habanos. Half of it belongs to the Cuban state. The other half has had three private owners since 2000. Around 2000, it went to Altadis, the Spanish-French tobacco group. In 2008, Britain's Imperial Tobacco bought Altadis. And in 2020, Imperial sold that half to a firm called Allied Cigar.

Allied's biggest owner, through companies he owns, is Chen Zhi, founder of Cambodia's Prince Group. In October 2025, the US and the UK sanctioned him, and US prosecutors charged him. The EU followed in July 2026. He is now in custody in China. He has denied wrongdoing, and the cigar companies have not been accused of any crime. Courts in the British Virgin Islands are still deciding who controls his holding companies there.

Across the water, the American twins belong to others. Montecristo, H. Upmann and Romeo y Julieta are sold by Altadis USA. Partagás, Hoyo de Monterrey and Punch are owned by Scandinavian Tobacco Group, the Danish company, which also sells the American Cohiba. US courts have ruled to cancel that Cohiba trademark. General Cigar, part of the same group, is appealing.

Here's the twist. For about twenty years, until 2020, one group owned half of Habanos and the American Montecristo too. One group on both sides of the water. Then it was sold in two separate deals, to holding companies whose owners weren't disclosed. halfwheel reported the same investors were said to be behind both, and that the American side has since been sold on. Today, according to halfwheel, the partner that owns the rest of Allied has ties to the firm that owns the American side.
