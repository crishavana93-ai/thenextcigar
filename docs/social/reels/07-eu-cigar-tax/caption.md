## Caption (paste)

The EU's plan to raise the minimum tax on cigars has stalled.

Today the EU floor is €12 per 1,000 cigars, or 5% of the retail price. In July 2025 the European Commission proposed €143 per 1,000, or 40%, almost twelve times higher.

The compromise put to finance ministers cut that to €80 per 1,000, or 30%, phased in by 2032 (€12 until 2029, €50 in 2030–31).

On 9 November 2026, 26 of the 27 countries backed that compromise. Malta withheld its consent, and tax rules need every country to agree. The next attempt is scheduled for 11 December.

In Sweden, cigars are already taxed above both figures: 1.83 kr per cigar in 2026. The government converted the Commission's €143 to about 1.70 kr.

Would a common EU minimum cigar tax be fair? Tell us in the comments.
