Three tax and law stories this week, all about cigars: the EU deal Malta blocked, Germany's new cigar tax, and a Belgian ban that has stalled. Plus a labelled Black Friday section for anyone buying accessories.

01  —  The EU cigar tax
European Quarter, Brussels. Video still: © European Union, 2026, CC BY 4.0

26 countries backed a higher EU cigar tax. Malta said no.

The EU minimum tax on cigars is €12 per 1,000, or 5% of the retail price. In July 2025 the European Commission proposed €143, or 40%. The compromise put to finance ministers cut that to €80 per 1,000, or 30%, phased in by 2032.

On 9 November, 26 of the 27 countries backed it. Malta withheld its consent, and tax rules need every country to agree, so the reform has stalled. In Sweden, cigars are already taxed above both figures: 1.83 kronor per cigar in 2026.

02  —  Germany
Reichstag building, Berlin. Photo: Christian David / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0

Germany will more than double its cigar tax.

Today each cigar or cigarillo in Germany pays 1.4 cents plus 1.47% of its retail price, or a minimum if that is higher. A law passed by the Bundestag on 15 October raises that from 1 March 2027 to 2.14 cents plus 3.83%, then every January to 2.85 cents in 2030. The minimum per piece goes from 7.504 to 13.964 cents, less the VAT in the price.

According to the German cigar makers' association, an early draft of the coalition's amendment would have pushed the price share to 21.05%. The law kept 3.83%, but with higher minimums than the government's draft. The association says 2.1 billion cigars and cigarillos were taxed in Germany in 2025, a third of the 2007 figure.

03  —  Belgium

Belgium's new smoking ban is on hold.

One Belgian draft law would close public smoking rooms, "such as cigar clubs or shisha bars", and ban smoking on café terraces from 1 January 2027. On 26 October the health minister's office said the Council of State had asked for changes to the draft law, so that date won't hold. Reports on the delay name the terrace ban; the smoking-room ban is in the same draft law, so it can't take effect until that law passes. No new date has been set. It is the second delay: the terrace ban was first planned for 1 January 2026.

Sources: Belga News Agency; VRT NWS and The Brussels Times, 26 Oct.
