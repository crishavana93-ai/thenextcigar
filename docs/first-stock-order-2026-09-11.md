# First stock order — real prices, real suppliers

**11 September 2026.** Prices read directly from Alibaba Ready-to-Ship on Cris's logged-in account, delivering to Sweden, in SEK as shown. Addendum to the accessories business plan.

---

## Why Ready-to-Ship changes the plan

Ordinary Alibaba listings want 500 pieces. That is the wall that stops most people testing a product, and it is why the earlier research came back with no usable prices — the public search shows the bulk listings, not the ones you can actually buy two of.

The Ready-to-Ship filter is a different catalogue: stock already made, sitting in a warehouse, minimum orders of one or two pieces. It is how you buy five of something to photograph and judge. The prices are higher per unit than a 500-piece order, so this is for **testing, not for margin** — the margin comes on the second order, once a product has proved it sells.

Two things jump out of that table.

**Changsha Ruitan appears twice with a minimum of one or two pieces.** Cases and ashtrays from one supplier means one conversation, one order, one parcel and one customs entry for a mixed test. Start there.

**The gap between the test price and the volume price is the whole business.** A leather case is SEK 79 at two pieces and SEK 36 at fifty. A cutter will fall similarly. So the first order is not meant to make money — it is meant to tell you which three products are worth a fifty-piece order.

---

## The first order

Buy samples, not stock. Something like:

- 5 × double-blade cutter (Hongjie) — about SEK 110
- 2 × 3-finger leather case (Ruitan) — about SEK 160
- 2 × ceramic ashtray (Ruitan) — about SEK 200
- 2 × custom-logo cutter (Terwin) — about SEK 375

Under SEK 900 plus shipping, from three suppliers, and at the end of it you will have handled every product and know which ones you would put your own name on. That is a cheap way to find out, and it is far cheaper than discovering it after buying fifty.

Ask every supplier three questions before ordering: the actual manufacturer name and address (you need it for the EU responsible-person rule), whether they can ship DDP to Sweden, and the unit price at 50 and at 100. The last one tells you whether the product has a business behind it.

---

## What to do with them when they arrive

Handle each one. Reject anything that cuts badly, feels hollow, or has a finish that will wear through in a month — a bad cutter costs you a reader, and readers are the expensive thing here.

Then photograph the survivors yourself, on paper, in daylight, in the newspaper's own visual language. Supplier photos on white backgrounds are the single clearest signal that a shop is a drop-shipping front, and your whole advantage is that yours is not.

---

## Costs to keep in the arithmetic

Since 1 July 2026 the EU applies a flat customs duty of **€3 per item** to consignments of €150 or under arriving from outside the EU, running until July 2028. On a sample order that is trivial. On a business that ships a €20 cutter direct from China to each customer it is fatal, which is the case for holding stock rather than drop-shipping, made again.

Import VAT is due on everything regardless of value, and Swedish VAT is 25%.
