# Blogs that rank and sell — SEO and content plan for the shop

*17 September 2026. Written after reading what actually ranks today for the queries our products answer (Google, US index, 17 Sept), the 60 articles we already have, and the 15 lines in the shop.*

## 1. What the search results say

Generic "how to" cigar queries are owned by American retailers with fifteen-year-old domains: Holt's, Famous Smoke, Cigar.com, Xikar, La Aurora. "How to cut a cigar", "how to fix a plugged cigar", "how to refill a torch lighter" are all theirs, page one is full, and their articles link to their own shops. We will not take those head-on and we should not try.

Three things they do not have, and we do:

1. **Europe.** Every one of those pages assumes the reader is in Ohio. Nobody ranks for "cigar cutter delivered EU no customs", "travel humidor Germany", "cigar lighter Sweden", "where to buy cigar accessories in Europe", or the same in the countries' own words. Our landed-cost angle (duties and VAT paid, nothing at the door) is a search phrase, not just a shop promise.
2. **Cuban specificity.** "Best cutter for a Cohiba Behike 56 ring gauge", "which lighter for a Montecristo No. 2 torpedo", "how to store Cubans at 65% not 70%". The US sites write about cigars in general; our readers came for Cubans, and Google's results for Cuban-specific accessory questions are thin.
3. **We already rank once.** `best-cigar-lighters-2026` is on page one for "best cigar lighter 2026" today, next to FashionBeans and 1stClassHumidors. That page currently points at Amazon affiliate picks. Pointing it at our own lighters is the single fastest revenue change in this plan.

## 2. The rule that makes it work

Every article in this plan is built around a **problem the reader has tonight** and a **product in our shop that solves it**, written by someone who has held the product. The product sits in the article as the answer, not as a banner: one "What we use" box after the paragraph where the problem is solved, with the photo, the price and a button, and once more at the end. No article is published about a product before the sample has been in your hands; until then the brief waits.

Google's current guidance rewards exactly this: first-hand experience (photos we took, tests we ran, numbers we measured), a clear author, and an article that answers the query in the first 150 words before it explains. Fluff, "in this article we will explore", and 2,000-word intros get demoted. Every brief below is 900 to 1,400 words.

## 4. Where the product goes inside an article

Three placements, no more:

- **The answer box**, right after the paragraph that solves the problem: photo, name, one line, price in USD with the € approximation, "Add to cart". Rendered from the product entry so the price is never stale.
- **The end box**, "What we used in this article", every product mentioned, in a row.
- **The inline link** on the first natural mention of the product's name.

Nothing else: no sidebar ads, no pop-ups, no "you might also like" carousels in the text. Readers came for the answer; the product is the answer.

## 5. Technical work that multiplies every article

In order of value, each one small:

1. **Google Merchant Center free listings.** A product feed of the 15 lines lets them show in Google's Shopping tab in SE, DE, UK for free. This is the largest source of product clicks we are not collecting. Needs a feed URL (I build it), a Merchant Center account (you create it, ten minutes) and the shipping/returns policy pages we already have.
2. **Product boxes in articles** (section 4), as an Astro component reading the product collection.
3. **FAQ schema** on the how-to articles: the three questions each article answers, marked up, which is what earns the expanded result on mobile.
4. **Author page** for you with the editorial standard ("held before reviewed") and a photo; Google's experience signals want a real person.
5. **Search Console**: resubmit the sitemap after the shop rebuild (the retired product URLs are gone) and watch "best cigar lighter" impressions as the rewrite lands.
6. **Internal links from the big traffic pages**: the Finder, the Germany shortage story and the box-code tool each get one line pointing to the article that fits (the shortage story → "the €40 humidor setup" for the boxes people are stockpiling; the box-code tool → hygrometer calibration).

## 6. Cadence and measurement

Two articles a week for six weeks covers the twelve. Article 1 (the lighter rewrite) the day the samples land; articles 2 to 4 the same week, because the tests take an evening each. The travel-humidor test (5) needs seven days, so it starts the day the case arrives and publishes the week after.

What we measure, weekly, in one line in the Weekend Edition planning note: Search Console clicks to each article, add-to-cart events from articles (the "Add to cart" button carries the article slug as source), orders by source. An article that gets clicks but no cart adds gets its answer box moved higher; one that gets neither after eight weeks gets rewritten or merged.

## 7. Social, the same content twice

Each article becomes one carousel (the test result as a chart or a photo sequence, in the paper's template) and one 30-second vertical video of the test itself: flame in wind, needle in the plug, hygrometer curve. Organic only; paid promotion of anything with "cigar" in it is rejected on TikTok and Meta. Every post ends on the article, never the product page, because the article carries the proof and the product box sits inside it.
