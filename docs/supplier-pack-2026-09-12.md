## What to write to them

Paste as-is, edit the bracketed parts. Keep it short — suppliers answer short messages faster, and a long one reads like a tyre-kicker.

### A. First contact (use this on every listing above)

> Hello,
>
> I run an online cigar-accessories shop in Sweden (thenextcigar.com) and I am selecting suppliers for our first range.
>
> Could you please send me:
>
> 1. Unit price for 10, 50 and 100 pieces, and your MOQ for each price break.
> 2. Total cost delivered DDP to Malmö, Sweden — including shipping and duty — for a sample order of [5] pieces.
> 3. Lead time for the sample, and for a 100-piece order.
> 4. The name and full address of the factory that manufactures this item. I need this for EU product-safety records (GPSR) — I cannot list a product without it.
> 5. Whether the item ships plain, without your logo or retail packaging.
>
> If the sample is good, I expect to reorder regularly.
>
> Thank you,
> Cristian Ortiz Suárez
> The Next Cigar, Sweden

Point 4 is the one that matters most and the one nobody asks. Since 13 December 2024 the EU requires a named, contactable responsible person for any product placed on the market, and you cannot be that person without knowing who actually made the thing. A supplier who won't give you a factory name and address is a supplier who is reselling something they can't vouch for either.

### B. If they answer with a price but skip the factory details

> Thank you for the price. Before I can order I still need the manufacturer's registered name and full address — this is a legal requirement for selling into the EU and I have to hold it on file for every item I list. Could you send it, and confirm whether you are the manufacturer or a trading company?

### C. Custom logo / own brand

> Thank you. I am also interested in the same item with our own logo.
>
> Could you tell me: the minimum quantity for custom logo, the additional cost per unit, the setup or mould fee if any, the logo methods available (laser, engraving, print), and the file format you need. Please also confirm the unit price with logo at 100 and at 300 pieces.

### D. Placing the sample order

> Please create an order for [5] pieces of [item] at [price], shipped DDP to Sweden. I will pay through Alibaba Trade Assurance.
>
> Please note on the parcel that it is a commercial sample and declare the true value — I need the customs paperwork to be correct.

Never pay a supplier outside Alibaba's Trade Assurance, however friendly the conversation gets, and however much cheaper a bank transfer sounds. The escrow is the only thing standing between you and a supplier who takes the money and stops replying.

### E. After the samples arrive — the reorder that gets you a real price

> The samples arrived and the quality is good. I would like to place an order for [100] pieces.
>
> At that quantity, what is your best unit price DDP to Sweden? I am comparing three suppliers on this item and will place the order this week with whoever gives the best combination of price and lead time. If this works well I will be ordering every [6-8] weeks.

Say it only if it's true. The comparison is real — that's why you opened three conversations.

### F. If the quality is poor

> Thank you for sending the sample. Unfortunately the [blade alignment / finish / spring tension] is not to the standard we need, so I will not be ordering this item. If you have a higher-grade version of the same product I would be happy to look at it.

Say it plainly and move on. Suppliers do not take it personally, and a polite no often produces a better product from the same factory.

## Before you place the first order

Ask yourself one thing per product: *would I use this myself, and would I be comfortable with a reader knowing I chose it?* Your shop's whole advantage is that it sits under a masthead people trust on Cuban cigars. A cutter that crushes the cap costs you a reader, and readers are the expensive thing here — far more expensive than the SEK 200 you saved on a bad batch.

Then photograph everything yourself, on paper, in daylight. Supplier photos on white backgrounds are the clearest possible signal that a shop is a drop-shipping front.

## Costs to keep in the arithmetic

Since 1 July 2026 the EU applies a flat customs duty of **€3 per item** on consignments of €150 or under from outside the EU, running until July 2028. Import VAT is due regardless of value. Swedish VAT is 25%. On a bulk order arriving as one consignment these are manageable; on single parcels drop-shipped to each customer they are the whole margin.
