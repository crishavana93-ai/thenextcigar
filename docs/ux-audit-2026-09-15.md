# thenextcigar.com — mobile and desktop UX audit

**15 September 2026, evening.** Method: scripted Chromium at iPhone 13 (390×844, touch) and 1440×900 desktop against the live site, seventeen public pages, measuring horizontal overflow, grid columns narrower than 150 px, tap targets under 32 px, text under 11 px, images without dimensions, console errors and page height. Then a hands-on pass on a phone (your screenshots) and in Chrome. The Lounge (signed-in) was audited separately the same day — see `qa-review-2026-09-15.md`.

## Verdict

The paper reads well on both. No page scrolls sideways at 390 px any more; body text is 17 px; the H1 scale (30 px phone, 48–121 px desktop) is right for a broadsheet; zero JavaScript errors on every page. The problems are the ones a print design meets on a phone: grids that keep their columns, print-size small caps, tap targets drawn for a mouse, and pages that are simply very long on a phone because every desktop module stacks.

## What to do about the long phone pages (M6)

Not a bug; a choice. Three options, cheapest first. (1) Collapse the secondary columns behind "More from the board ▾" / "More in the magazine ▾" disclosures on phones only, default closed — cuts home to ~4 screens. (2) A phone-only front page: lead story, the board's top six, three magazine heads, the shop's four; everything else one tap away. (3) Leave it; a long scroll is how a newspaper site reads on a phone and the numbers (985 clicks/28 d, cutters guide +111%) say readers scroll. I'd do (1) for the home and board, nothing for articles.

## Passed

No horizontal overflow on any page at 390 or 320 px. Body copy 17 px. Colour contrast: ink on cream passes AAA; oxblood on cream passes AA at the sizes used. All images have `alt`; all pages one H1. Every article's `metaTitle`/`metaDescription` now reach the `<head>` (fixed 15 Sep). Ticker pauses on hover and respects reduced motion.

## The oxblood chip

Added today as `.chip`: the solid oxblood block with cream small caps from the Instagram covers, on the category label above every article headline, the lead kicker on the front page and the lead on the magazine page. One per block. It is the accent doing its job; it should not spread to section heads, nav or buttons, or the paper turns red.
