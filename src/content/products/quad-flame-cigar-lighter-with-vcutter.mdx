The argument for a combination tool is simple: the thing you forget is never the lighter, it is the cutter. Putting the cutter in the base of the lighter means there is only one object to remember, and it is the object you were always going to bring.

Three jets for the light, a V-cutter underneath, and a notch on the top to rest the cigar on while you pour something. That last part sounds like a gimmick until the first time you have nowhere to put a lit cigar down.

## About the V-cut

A V-cut takes a wedge out of the cap rather than slicing it flat. It opens more draw surface without removing as much of the cap, which is why it suits a tightly rolled cigar that draws hard — and why it is the wrong cut for a very thin ring gauge, where there is not enough cap to take a wedge out of.

If you only ever smoke robustos, a straight cut is fine and this is the wrong tool. If you smoke a mixture and have ever fought a tight draw, this is the one.

## It arrives empty

**No gas in it.** A lighter holding butane is UN 1057, dangerous goods class 2.1, and cannot travel by ordinary air freight. Purged and empty it is just metal, which is how every lighter bought online crosses a border.

Fill it yourself with any refined butane refill — a few euros in any tobacconist, and it lasts months.
