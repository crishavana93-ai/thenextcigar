**In short:** On **21 October 2026**, Justice Gerhard Wallbank of the High Court of the British Virgin Islands (BVI) announced that he will dismiss the applications to wind up **30 companies** linked to Chen Zhi and end the court-appointed liquidation that has run them since January. One of those companies is **Simply Advanced Limited**, which holds Chen's indirect stake in Habanos S.A., the company that sells and distributes Cuban cigars worldwide. No order has been made yet. The written judgment is expected after 6 November, and a further hearing on how the liquidators hand over is provisionally set for **8–9 December**. The ruling does not lift any sanctions. Trade press in Europe warns it could make banks more cautious, not less.

*Reported by halfwheel and Cigars-connect on 4 November 2026. The Prince Group says Chen Zhi is innocent and denies the allegations against him.*

## What the court decided

In January, at the request of the BVI's Attorney General, the court put more than 30 companies tied to Chen Zhi under provisional liquidation. The liquidators, from the advisory firm Interpath, took control of them with the aim of stabilising and eventually selling them. Chen's side, represented by the accountant Cosimo Borrelli, challenged that.

According to halfwheel, which published extracts of the judge's spoken remarks, Justice Wallbank found that the court had not been given admissible evidence to justify winding the companies up. He said the US indictment and forfeiture complaint were not evidence in themselves and that "mere association is not enough", and concluded that "only one outcome is open to the Court". He also said the decision should be implemented in an orderly way, warning that a sudden handover could cost jobs and close businesses.

Cigars-connect notes that the ruling does not address the merits of the US allegations against Chen Zhi.

## Why it matters for Cuban cigars

Habanos S.A. is owned 50% by the Cuban state and 50% by Allied Cigar Corporation, a Madrid company. Halfwheel reports, based on an ownership chart published by Cigarrvärlden, that Chen Zhi indirectly owns 57.1% of Allied Cigar through a chain of companies on three continents, which works out at **28.55% of Habanos S.A.** That makes him its second-largest shareholder after the Cuban state. The remaining 42.9% of Allied Cigar is held by Renovaire Group Holding, a company with ties to Abu Dhabi's ruling family. Allied Cigar's group also holds stakes in Cuban cigar distributors around the world. We explain the full chain in [Who owns Habanos, and why sanctions matter](/blog/habanos-chen-zhi-ownership-sanctions-explained/).

Since January, Chen did not control that stake; the liquidators did. If the ruling is carried out, Chen's lawyers want control to pass to Borrelli, whom they say Chen appointed; Cigars-connect reports his representatives declined to provide the documents showing that authority. Chen's ownership, and the sanctions on him, would not change.

## Why importers are worried

The sanctions have already stopped Cuban cigars in parts of Europe. The German importer for Germany, Austria and Poland stopped deliveries in early September; its administrator told Cigars-connect it was "insolvent… not because it cannot pay, but because it is not allowed to", and formal insolvency proceedings opened on 1 November. In Spain, Cigars-connect reports that since the EU sanctions every payment by the importer, Tabacalera, needs prior clearance from the Spanish Treasury. We covered the German shortage in [Germany has run out of Cuban cigars](/blog/germany-cuban-cigar-shortage-september-2026/).

Writing for Cigars-connect, Laurent Mimouni of the French magazine L'Amateur de Cigare argues that a director appointed by Chen could make banks even more reluctant to deal with these companies, and that the sanctions licences granted to the UK and US distributors, Hunters & Frankau and Tabacalera USA, could come under review. His conclusion: "Chen Zhi's legal victory will not unblock the bank accounts of Cuban cigar distributors."

The other side sees it differently. The Prince Group says Chen is innocent, and a spokesperson told halfwheel: "Right now, the focus is on restoring control of the business where it belongs. Future decisions about ownership are pre-mature."

## It is not over yet

- **A possible stay.** Lawyers for the liquidators told a New York court that they understand the BVI Attorney General intends to seek a stay of any order ending the liquidation. Halfwheel says it is unclear whether that has been filed.
- **US courts.** On 2 November, Borrelli's lawyers asked a New York court to end the liquidators' powers in the US. The liquidators' lawyers called the attempt premature.
- **A sale.** In May, Interpath's Paul Pretlove said in a deposition that several parties had approached him about buying the stake. Renovaire has said it is pursuing Chen's "total dissociation" from Allied Cigar, including buying his stake. Nothing has been announced.

## Sweden and the Nordics

Sweden, Norway, Denmark, Finland, Iceland and the Baltic states have been without an official Habanos importer since the Swedish licence of Elite Trading Scandinavia was lost for good on 11 September. Nothing in this ruling changes that. The background is in [Sweden's Cuban cigar importer is being wound up](/blog/sweden-cuban-cigar-importer-liquidation-2026/).
