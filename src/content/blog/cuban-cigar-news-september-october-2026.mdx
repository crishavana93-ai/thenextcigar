**In short:** September and October 2026 were the two months when the ownership problems at Habanos S.A. reached European shop shelves. Germany's importer stopped deliveries on 4 September and filed for insolvency; Sweden's importer lost its licence for good on 11 September. At the same time Habanos kept launching cigars: the Cohiba Talismán became a regular cigar in London on 7 October, and the Quai d'Orsay Concordia, the only Edición Limitada announced for 2026, premiered in Madrid on 24 October. In Brussels, talks on new EU tobacco taxes continued.

We picked these two months because the European supply story starts on 4 September and its latest reporting is from late October.

## 1. Germany's importer stops, then files for insolvency

5th Avenue Products Trading GmbH, based in Waldshut-Tiengen, is the official Habanos importer for **Germany, Austria and Poland**. On 4 September it suspended deliveries; Finews reported that its bank accounts had been frozen. A week later the company confirmed it had filed for insolvency, and a court in Waldshut-Tiengen appointed an administrator.

According to Cigar Aficionado, shops, including Casa del Habano locations in Germany, can sell the stock they hold but have no new supply while the review runs. Cigar Aficionado reports that 80% of 5th Avenue is owned by Altabana, part of the Prince Holding Group network. No new importer has been named. Background: [the Cuban cigar shortage in Germany](/blog/germany-cuban-cigar-shortage-september-2026/).

## 2. Sweden's importer is gone for good

Elite Trading Scandinavia (formerly Habanos Nordic), which imported Cuban cigars for the Nordics and the Baltics, had its wholesale licence revoked by Gothenburg in December 2025. It appealed and lost, and the licence was definitively gone on **11 September 2026**, Realtid reported on 22 October. Its shareholders had already voted on 1 July for voluntary liquidation.

The trade site Cigarrvärlden estimates the company held about 45% of the Swedish cigar market. Realtid reports that Cuban cigars are now hard to find in Sweden, though smaller importers and private imports remain. No successor has been named. Full story: [Sweden's Cuban cigar importer is being wound up](/blog/sweden-cuban-cigar-importer-liquidation-2026/).

## 3. Why both are happening: the Habanos ownership problem

Half of Habanos S.A. is owned by the Cuban state. The other half was sold by Imperial Brands in 2020 to Allied Cigar Corporation, which Cigar Aficionado reports belongs to Prince Holding Group. Its founder, Chen Zhi, was indicted in the US in October 2025 and has been sanctioned by the US, the UK and the EU; the EU sanctions date from July 2026. Prince Group denies the allegations.

Both importers' problems trace back to it. Realtid reports that the Swedish court ruled Gothenburg's licence review had to take Chen Zhi's stake into account, and Cigar Aficionado ties 5th Avenue's halt to the case against Chen Zhi and Prince Holding Group. More: [Habanos S.A.'s ownership change explained](/blog/habanos-sa-unpacking-the-landmark-ownership-change/).

## 4. The Cohiba Talismán becomes a regular cigar

On 7 October in London, Habanos made the Cohiba Talismán, a 2017 limited edition, the ninth size in Cohiba's Línea Clásica: 154 mm, 54 ring gauge, boxes of 10. It opened Cohiba's 60th anniversary year. Habanos said boxes would reach markets gradually over the coming year. Details: [Cohiba Talismán becomes a regular cigar](/blog/cohiba-talisman-regular-production-2026/).

## 5. The year's only Edición Limitada (so far)

On 24 October at the Las Ventas bullring in Madrid, Habanos premiered the **Quai d'Orsay Concordia Edición Limitada 2026**: 145 mm, 50 ring gauge, boxes of 20 at a recommended €914. Habanos usually releases about three Limited Editions a year; so far this is the only one announced for 2026. Spain gets it first. Details: [Quai d'Orsay Concordia explained](/blog/quai-dorsay-concordia-edicion-limitada-2026/).

## 6. EU tobacco tax talks continue

The EU is revising its tobacco excise rules, including minimum taxes on cigars and cigarillos. Changes need the agreement of every member state. The Council's high-level tax working party was scheduled to discuss new compromise texts from the presidency on 26 October. No agreement has been announced. Our earlier explainer: [how the EU directive could change cigar prices](/blog/the-tax-man-cometh-a-new-eu-directive-could-change-cigar-prices-forever/).

## What to watch in November

- Who, if anyone, takes over Cuban imports for Germany, Austria, Poland and the Nordics.
- When the Talismán and the Concordia reach shops outside the UK and Spain.
- Whether EU finance ministers reach a deal on tobacco taxes.
