A travel case has one job, and it is not to look good on a hotel dresser. It is to hand you, on Sunday night, the same cigar you packed on Friday morning. Most cases fail at that quietly: the cigar arrives whole, lights, and tastes flat, and the case gets the credit while the cigar gets the blame. So this guide judges cases by what happens inside them, and only then by how they look.

There are three kinds. Which one is right depends on how long you are away and how you travel, and we would rather you buy the cheap one that fits than the handsome one that doesn't.

## The cedar rule

Whatever the outside is made of, the inside should be Spanish cedar, or the cigars should be in something that is. Cedar is what humidors are lined with for a reason: it holds a little moisture, it keeps the smell of leather, glue and dye off the wrapper, and it stops three cigars pressed together for two days from trading flavours. A leather case with a bare leather interior does none of that, and after a weekend a Partagás can come out tasting faintly of shoe.

If a case does not say what it is lined with, assume nothing.

## Leather finger cases — for a weekend

The classic: two or three "fingers" of leather, telescoping, a cigar in each. The good ones are cedar-lined inside the fingers; the ones to avoid are lined with more leather or with nothing.

What it does well: it goes in a jacket pocket, it protects against bending, and for one to three days a cedar-lined finger holds a cigar's humidity close enough that you will not taste the difference. What it does not do: it is not sealed. On day four in a dry hotel room the cigars start to go, and a summer car does the same in an afternoon.

How many fingers: three. Two is one short of a weekend; five stays at home because it does not fit a pocket.

The one we'd buy: the [AMANCY three-finger case](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B072J42G5Q?tag=thenextcigar-20), which is cedar-lined inside the fingers and rated 4.8 stars by more than 5,000 owners ($22.79 on Amazon.com, 21 October 2026).

## Tube cases — for the bag, not the pocket

A tube case is a set of individual sealed tubes in a pouch or a hard shell. Each cigar rides in its own cylinder, which means it cannot be crushed by whatever else is in the bag, and it cannot share air with its neighbours.

The tube trap is humidity. A sealed tube holds whatever the cigar brought with it: a properly stored cigar stays right for several days, an over-humidified one sweats, and a dry one stays dry. A tube is a container, not a humidor. For anything over four or five days, put a small humidity pack in the pouch, not in the tubes.

For a single sealed tube, the [Defuzie aluminum tube](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CB57PTDK?tag=thenextcigar-20) ($10.99, 4.8 stars from 274 ratings) has a screw cap and fits most Robustos and Coronas; check the length against your longest cigar before you buy.

The other thing to know is that "tube" means two different things on a listing. A metal or plastic travel tube with a screw cap is a case component. A Habanos factory tubo is packaging the cigar was sold in, and it does the same job for one cigar at no extra cost, which is why the tubed versions of a Romeo No. 1 or a Bolívar Tubos are a traveller's cigar to begin with.

## Hard travel humidors — for a week or more

A crush-proof shell with a foam or cedar interior and a humidity source. These are the only cases that actually keep cigars for a week, and they are the ones to fly with if the cigars are worth more than the case. They are also the ones that go in checked luggage, because they are the size of a book.

The trade: weight, size, and the temptation to over-humidify a sealed box. Set the pack to 65 or 69 per cent, not 72, and open the lid for a minute each day.

## The airport, in one paragraph

Cigars are not a problem at security. Lighters are: a torch lighter is not allowed in cabin baggage on most carriers and will be taken at the checkpoint (in the US, the TSA bans torch lighters from checked bags too), while a soft-flame lighter usually rides in a pocket. Matches and a cutter with a blade under six centimetres are normally fine in the cabin, but a guillotine can be judged a blade by an officer having a bad day; the punch never is. And the number of cigars you can bring home without paying duty is a separate question, which is what the [traveller's allowance calculator](/tools/allowance/) is for.

## How to choose, in three questions

How many nights? Up to three, leather fingers. Four to seven, tubes with a humidity pack. More, a hard humidor.

Pocket or bag? Only leather fingers go in a jacket. Everything else is luggage.

What is the cigar worth? A case should cost less than the cigars it carries. If it doesn't, you are buying leather, and that is fine, as long as you know it.
