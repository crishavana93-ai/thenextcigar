Eight books about the island where Cuban tobacco is grown: its history, its capital, the history of tobacco itself, and the two novels most readers know Cuba by. For handbooks about cigars themselves, see [the best cigar books to give](/blog/best-cigar-books/).

We chose them on two things: whether the book is well regarded by people who know the subject (prizes, publishers, how long it has stayed in print) and how readers rate it. Every title, author, publisher and date below was checked against the book's Amazon listing on **8 November 2026**, and every prize against the prize body or the author's university. Six of the eight are also on Audible; we list the narrator, and the running time where Amazon shows it.

## The history

### 1. Cuba: An American History, by Ada Ferrer

A widely praised modern history of the island, from the Spanish conquest to the present, told through Cuba's long and tangled relationship with the United States. Ferrer, a Havana-born historian now at Princeton (formerly New York University), won the **2022 Pulitzer Prize for History** for it.

Scribner, 2021, 576 pages. **4.6 stars from 2,597 ratings.**
**Audiobook:** narrated by Alma Cuervo, 23 hours 13 minutes.

[See Cuba: An American History on Amazon →](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1501154559?tag=thenextcigar-20) · [Audiobook →](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08MWSP641?tag=thenextcigar-20)

### 2. Havana: A Subtropical Delirium, by Mark Kurlansky

A shorter, looser portrait of the city: its history, music, food and baseball, from a writer best known for *Cod* and *Salt*. A good book to read before a first trip.

Bloomsbury, 2017 (paperback 2018), 272 pages. **4.3 stars from 254 ratings.**
**Audiobook:** narrated by Fleet Cooper, 6 hours 1 minute.

[See Havana on Amazon →](https://www.amazon.com/dp/1632863928?tag=thenextcigar-20) · [Audiobook →](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01MZC1HQO?tag=thenextcigar-20)

### 3. Havana Nocturne, by T. J. English

How American organised crime ran Havana's casinos and hotels in the 1950s, and lost them to the revolution in 1959. Narrative history that reads like a crime novel.

William Morrow, paperback 2009, 396 pages. **4.5 stars from 1,590 ratings.**

[See Havana Nocturne on Amazon →](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0061712744?tag=thenextcigar-20)

## Tobacco

### 4. Cuban Counterpoint: Tobacco and Sugar, by Fernando Ortiz

The classic. Ortiz, the Cuban anthropologist, first published it in 1940: a study of Cuban history and society through its two great crops, set against each other like two voices in music. It is also the book in which he introduced the idea of "transculturation". The English edition is Harriet de Onís's translation, with Bronisław Malinowski's original introduction, a prologue by Herminio Portell Vilá and a newer introduction by Fernando Coronil.

Duke University Press, 1995 edition, 408 pages. **4.6 stars from 23 ratings.** Not on Audible.

[See Cuban Counterpoint on Amazon →](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0822316161?tag=thenextcigar-20)

### 5. Tobacco: A Cultural History, by Iain Gately

The whole story of the plant, from its use in the Americas before Columbus to the modern cigar and cigarette, and how it spread around the world. Broad and readable rather than academic.

Grove Press, 2001 (paperback 2003), 416 pages. **4.5 stars from 236 ratings.**
**Audiobook:** narrated by Christopher Grove, 12 hours 35 minutes.

[See Tobacco on Amazon →](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0802139604?tag=thenextcigar-20) · [Audiobook →](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B013V9YB6K?tag=thenextcigar-20)

## A life

### 6. Waiting for Snow in Havana, by Carlos Eire

A memoir of a childhood in Havana before and after the revolution, which ends with the author, aged eleven, sent to the United States in 1962 without his parents, with his older brother, through Operation Pedro Pan. Eire, a historian at Yale, won the **National Book Award for Nonfiction in 2003** for it.

Free Press, paperback 2004, 400 pages. **4.2 stars from 1,626 ratings.**
**Audiobook:** narrated by David Drummond (Tantor Audio), on Audible.

[See Waiting for Snow in Havana on Amazon →](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0743246411?tag=thenextcigar-20)

## Two novels

### 7. The Old Man and the Sea, by Ernest Hemingway

Santiago, an old Cuban fisherman, and his fight with a great marlin in the Gulf Stream. Hemingway was living outside Havana when it was published in 1952; it and won the **Pulitzer Prize for Fiction in 1953**. Short enough to finish in one evening.

Scribner, 128 pages. **4.7 stars from 10,884 ratings.**
**Audiobook:** narrated by Donald Sutherland, 2 hours 28 minutes.

[See The Old Man and the Sea on Amazon →](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0684801221?tag=thenextcigar-20) · [Audiobook →](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B000FIHM3U?tag=thenextcigar-20)

### 8. Our Man in Havana, by Graham Greene

Greene's 1958 comedy: a vacuum-cleaner salesman in Havana is recruited by British intelligence and, short of real secrets, starts inventing them. The Penguin Classics edition has an introduction by Christopher Hitchens.

Penguin Classics, 256 pages. **4.2 stars from 552 ratings.**
**Audiobook:** narrated by Jeremy Northam, 7 hours 5 minutes.

## Quick answers

**What is a good book on the history of Cuba?** For a single modern history, Ada Ferrer's *Cuba: An American History*, which won the 2022 Pulitzer Prize for History.

**Is there a classic book about Cuban tobacco?** Fernando Ortiz's *Cuban Counterpoint: Tobacco and Sugar* (1940) is still widely cited by scholars, above all for the idea of transculturation.

**Which Hemingway book is set in Cuba?** *The Old Man and the Sea* (1952) follows a Cuban fisherman out of a village near Havana. *Islands in the Stream*, published after his death, is partly set in Cuba too.
