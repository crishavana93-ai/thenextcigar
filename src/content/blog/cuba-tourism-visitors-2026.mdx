**In short:** Cuba received **477,707 international visitors between January and September 2026**, according to its national statistics office, ONEI. In the same months of 2025 it received 1,367,847. That is **890,140 fewer people, a fall of 65.1%**. September, with 27,354 arrivals, was the lowest month of the year so far.

*Figures: ONEI, as reported by EFE and CiberCuba on 28–29 October 2026, by CiberCuba on 24 September, and by Bloomberg Línea and The Rio Times on 2 September. We could not open ONEI's own site to read the bulletins directly, so every figure here is as those outlets reported it. Where two outlets differ, we say so.*

## The numbers, month by month

ONEI publishes a running total each month. Three have been widely reported since the summer:

| Period | Visitors, 2026 | Same period, 2025 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| January–July | 419,863 | 1,128,831 | −62.8% |
| January–August | 450,353 | 1,264,332 | −64.4% |
| January–September | 477,707 | 1,367,847 | −65.1% |

The 2025 column is worked out from the losses ONEI gives (708,968, 813,979 and 890,140 fewer visitors). The gap is still widening: each new month has been weaker than the one before it.

The single months show how thin arrivals have become:

- **June:** 28,100
- **July:** 32,272, about half of July 2025
- **August:** 30,490, against about 133,000 in August 2025
- **September:** 27,354, the lowest of the year

ONEI also publishes a wider count of "travellers", which adds Cubans who live on the island returning home and airline crew. That stood at 970,382 for January to September.

## Why some headlines say 35% and others 65%

ONEI reports each total as a share of the year before. For January to September that share is 34.9%: this year's visitors are 34.9% of last year's. Several outlets, including the EFE report carried by Infobae, wrote that as a fall of 34.9%. It is not. If 34.9% remain, **65.1% are gone**.

The same slip happened with the July figures, where 37.2% (the share that remained) was reported in places as the fall. The real fall to July was 62.8%. The Rio Times pointed this out in its own report.

| Cubans living abroad | 117,386 | 60,081 fewer |
| United States | 45,895 | 42,984 fewer |
| Mexico | 24,369 | about −45% |

Canada is the story. It was Cuba's largest market by far, and it has lost more than three visitors in four. Canada alone accounts for almost half of the 890,140 missing arrivals.

## What is reported as the cause

The outlets agree on what has happened and differ on what they stress.

- **Fuel and power.** CiberCuba points to fuel shortages, supply problems and fewer flights. It reported blackouts of more than 24 hours and at least eight collapses of the national grid this year, and travel advisories from Canada, the United States, the United Kingdom, Argentina and Ireland.
- **Flights.** A shortage of jet fuel led Air Canada, WestJet, Air Transat and Sunwing to suspend or cut routes, CiberCuba reported. Bloomberg Línea reported that Air Canada and WestJet had suspended flights since early 2026.
- **United States measures.** EFE attributes the fall to US pressure: what it calls an oil blockade since January and further sanctions from May. Bloomberg Línea reported that Visa and Mastercard stopped working with Fincimex, a company in the military-run GAESA group, citing Cuba's central bank.
- **Hotels.** The Cuban government acknowledged in July that seven international operators managing 46% of the country's hotel rooms, more than 80,000, had left, EFE reported. They include Meliá, Iberostar and Barceló. Prime Minister Manuel Marrero Cruz said in July that 73% of hotel facilities were closed, according to CiberCuba.

## How far this is from normal

| Year | International visitors |
|---|---|
| 2018 | about 4.6 million (the peak) |
| 2019 | 4.2 million |
| 2023 | 2.4 million |
| 2024 | 2.2 million |
| 2025 | about 1.8 million |
| 2026, to September | 477,707 |

Full-year figures as reported by EFE. 2025 was already Cuba's weakest year since 2002 outside the pandemic, and it fell short of a state forecast of 2.6 million. With three months left, 2026 is on course to finish at a fraction of it.

The government approved a package of 176 reform measures in June, EFE reported, and CiberCuba reported changes allowing private businesses to run hotels, car rental, transport and guiding.

## What this means for Cuban cigars, and what it doesn't

None of the reports mentions tobacco or cigars, and ONEI's visitor count says nothing about cigar sales. So this section is short, and it separates what is known from what is not.

**What is known.** Visitors are one of the ways Cuban cigars are sold: travellers buy them in Cuba's state-run shops and take them home. Fewer than half a million visitors in nine months means far fewer people passing through those shops than in any recent year. The Festival del Habano, the trade's annual gathering in Havana, did not take place in 2026; [Cigar Aficionado reported its cancellation](https://www.cigaraficionado.com/article/habanos-festival-officially-canceled).

**What is not known.** No figure has been published for cigar sales inside Cuba in 2026, and Habanos S.A. has not commented on these tourism numbers. The fuel shortages and blackouts reported above affect the whole economy, but none of the reports we read says how they have affected tobacco farming or the factories. We will not guess.

**What matters more for buyers in Europe** is not tourism but distribution: the sanctions on one of Habanos S.A.'s indirect shareholders, the insolvency of the German importer and the liquidation of the Nordic one. We cover those in [who owns Habanos S.A.](/blog/habanos-chen-zhi-ownership-sanctions-explained/), [the BVI court ruling](/blog/bvi-court-chen-zhi-habanos-stake-ruling-2026/) and [Sweden's lost importer](/blog/sweden-cuban-cigar-importer-liquidation-2026/).

## Quick answers

**How many tourists visited Cuba in 2026?** 477,707 international visitors from January to September 2026, according to ONEI, down 65.1% from 1,367,847 in the same months of 2025.

**Why has tourism to Cuba fallen?** Reports point to fuel shortages and long blackouts, airlines suspending routes, US sanctions and the departure of foreign hotel operators. Outlets differ on which they stress.

**Which country sends the most visitors to Cuba?** Canada, with 129,243 visitors in January–September 2026, though that is 76.9% fewer than a year earlier.

**Did tourism fall 35% or 65%?** 65.1%. The 34.9% in some headlines is the share of last year's visitors that remained, not the fall.
