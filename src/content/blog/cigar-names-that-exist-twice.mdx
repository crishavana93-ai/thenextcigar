**In short:** If you have seen a Montecristo, a Partagás or a Romeo y Julieta that says "Dominican Republic" or "Honduras", it isn't a fake. At least ten famous names exist twice: one version made in Cuba, sold everywhere except the United States, and one made elsewhere and sold in the US. The split goes back to 1960, when Cuba's new government seized the leading cigar makers, and to a 1972 US court ruling that let several exiled owners keep their trademarks in America.

## How the split happened

- **1960:** "the Cuban Government confiscated the business and assets of the five leading manufacturers of Havana cigars," the US Supreme Court later recorded. Most of their owners "had fled to the United States" ([*Alfred Dunhill of London v. Republic of Cuba*, 425 U.S. 682](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/425/682)).
- **1962:** Proclamation 3447 banned "the importation into the United States of all goods of Cuban origin" from 7 February 1962 ([National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/federal-register/codification/proclamations/03447.html)).
- **1972:** in *Menendez v. Faber, Coe & Gregg*, a federal court in New York found that the exiled owners of five Cuban firms still owned their US registered trademarks: they "did not lose their rights to these trademarks by reason of the suspension of their businesses" ([345 F.Supp. 527](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp/345/527/1891218/)).

The result, as *Cigar Aficionado* puts it, is that the non-Cuban "parallel brands could only be sold in the United States," while the Cuban versions are shipped everywhere else ([Cigar Aficionado, 2018](https://www.cigaraficionado.com/article/what-s-in-a-brand-name)).

## The 10 names

|---|---|---|---|
| Montecristo | Menéndez, García y Cía. (Alonso Menéndez, Pepe García) | Canary Islands, then the Dominican Republic | Altadis U.S.A.; mostly Dominican, some Nicaraguan |
| H. Upmann | Founded by banker Hermann Upmann; later Menéndez, García y Cía. | Canary Islands | Altadis U.S.A.; Honduras and Dominican Republic |
| Partagás | Cifuentes, Fernández y Cía. (Ramón Cifuentes) | Jamaica, under Cifuentes; later the Dominican Republic | General Cigar; Dominican Republic |
| Hoyo de Monterrey | Fernández, Palicio y Cía. (Fernando Palicio) | Sold to Villazon & Co.; Honduras by 1965 | General Cigar; mostly Honduras |
| Punch | Fernández, Palicio y Cía. | Villazon; Tampa first, then Honduras | General Cigar; mostly Honduras |
| Romeo y Julieta | Rodríguez, Argüelles y Cía. (built up by José "Don Pepín" Rodríguez; run by his son by 1960) | Kept alive by Hollco Rohr with machine-made cigars in Kingston, New York; handmade in the Dominican Republic from 1978 | Altadis; Dominican Republic and Nicaragua |
| La Gloria Cubana | Cifuentes family from 1954, per Cigar Aficionado; Ernesto Pérez-Carrillo says his father had bought the rights in Cuba | El Credito factory, Miami (from 1974); Dominican Republic from 1996 | General Cigar; Dominican Republic |
| Bolívar | J.F. Rocha y Cía.; Cifuentes y Cía. from 1954 | Trademark sold to General Cigar, registered 1983 | General Cigar |
| Trinidad | Disputed. Cuba says its Trinidad was created after the revolution, in the 1960s, as a diplomatic gift; the Trinidad family held a 1958 "TTT Trinidad" trademark and won the US rights in 2001 | Made by the Fuente factory in Tampa from 1968; Dominican Republic from 1997 | Altadis U.S.A. (bought from the Trinidad family in 2002); Dominican Republic |
| Cohiba | **None.** Created in Cuba in 1966, never seized from a private owner | General Cigar filed for the US trademark in 1978, registered 1981; Dominican-made US version from 1994 | General Cigar. The US trademark board ordered its registrations cancelled; a federal court upheld that in May 2025, and General Cigar has appealed |

*Source for the table: Gregory Mottola, "What's in a Brand Name?", Cigar Aficionado, January/February 2018; Cohiba cancellation: Finnegan, "Incontestable" (2025). Company names are as reported in 2018; ownership can change. More names exist in two versions, including several covered by the 1972 ruling.*

## How the two versions are labelled

A Cuban cigar sold as a Habano carries the protected denomination of origin **Habano**. Since 1994, every box shipped from Cuba has carried the Habanos seal, and boxes are stamped "Hecho en Cuba" and, for hand-made cigars, "Totalmente a mano". The government warranty seal dates back to 1889 ([Habanos S.A.](https://www.habanos.com/en/origen-denomination/)). The non-Cuban versions say where they are made, usually the Dominican Republic, Honduras or Nicaragua. Those three countries made more than 99% of the handmade cigars the US imported in January–September 2025 ([Tobacco Reporter, CAA data](https://tobaccoreporter.com/2026/01/16/u-s-cigar-imports-up-4-6-in-2025/)).

This is about names and history, not quality. Neither version is "the real one": both are legal products from different owners under different laws.
