## How to actually pick

**1. Indoors or outdoors?** Mostly indoors: a single jet (Xikar EX or Colibri Rally). Outdoors more than half the time: a double or triple jet (Xikar ELX or PROMISE).

**2. What ring gauge do you usually smoke?** Coronas, Robustos and Toros (roughly 42–54): a single jet handles them. Gordos and anything over 56: a double or triple jet lights them faster and more evenly.

**3. Pocket or table?** For a pocket, any of the torches. For the table next to the humidor, the Volta.

If you smoke more than once a week, two lighters is a small upgrade: one at home, one in the car or travel case.

## Single, double or triple jet?

**Single jet:** one narrow flame. Toasts the foot evenly on most cigars, uses the least butane, and is the easiest to control on a Corona or a Petit Robusto. The Xikar EX and the Colibri Rally.

**Double jet:** two flames side by side. Faster on 56-plus ring gauges and steadier in wind, at the cost of more fuel. The Xikar ELX.

**Triple and quad jet:** the most heat. They light a Gordo or a Behike 56 quickly and keep going in a breeze, but can over-toast thin cigars unless you hold them further away. The PROMISE (triple) and the Volta (quad).

If you smoke mostly Cuban Robustos and Coronas indoors, a single jet is all you need.

## Questions people ask

**What is the best cigar lighter?** For most smokers, a single-jet torch from a maker with a real warranty. Our pick is the Xikar EX. Go double-jet (Xikar ELX) if you smoke outdoors or above a 56 ring gauge.

**What's the best cheap cigar lighter?** The PROMISE triple jet: $15.99, with 4.4 stars from more than 2,000 Amazon ratings.

**Is a torch lighter better than matches or a soft flame?** A torch is faster and works in wind. Cedar spills and long matches give a gentler, slower light that some smokers prefer for an expensive cigar. A soft-flame lighter sits in between.

**Why won't my torch lighter light?** Usually air in the tank, a flame set too low, a dirty jet, or the cold. Our [torch lighter troubleshooting guide](/blog/why-your-torch-lighter-wont-light/) goes through each cause and fix.

**Can I fly with a torch lighter?** In the United States, no: the TSA does not allow torch lighters in carry-on or checked bags ([tsa.gov](https://www.tsa.gov/travel/security-screening/whatcanibring/items/torch-lighters)). Rules elsewhere differ by airline and country, so check before you pack.

## Looking after a torch lighter

- **Bleed the tank before refilling.** Air trapped in the tank is a common cause of weak flames and failed ignition, and Xikar advises bleeding before every refill. The steps are in our [troubleshooting guide](/blog/why-your-torch-lighter-wont-light/).
- **Use refined butane.** Cheap butane can leave residue that clogs the jet. [Xikar Purofine](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B06XDF6QYM?tag=thenextcigar-20) is the refined fuel Xikar sells for its own lighters.
- **Use the warranty.** Xikar covers its lighters for life against manufacturing defects ([xikar.com/warranty](https://xikar.com/warranty)).
