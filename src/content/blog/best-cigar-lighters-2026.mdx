## How to actually pick

Three questions to ask yourself before you buy:

**1. Indoors or outdoors?** Mostly indoors → single-jet (Xikar EX or Trezo). Outdoors more than half the time → double-jet (Xikar ELX). The wind matters; choose accordingly.

**2. What ring gauge do you usually smoke?** Coronas, Robustos, Toros (42–54 RG) → single-jet handles them fine. Gordos, Magnums, anything over 56 RG → double-jet gets you a faster, more even light.

**3. Pocket or desk?** Both options work for most lighters here. But the Xikar Trezo specifically excels at pocket carry (slim, weighted), while the Ultra Magnetic specifically excels at desk use (magnetic mount). Match the form factor to your actual habit.

If you smoke more than once a week, **owning two lighters is a small upgrade with disproportionate convenience**: keep one at home, one in your travel humidor or car. The 30 seconds of "where did I put it?" you save on every smoke adds up.

## Single, double or triple jet?

**Single jet:** one narrow flame. Toasts the foot evenly on anything up to about 54 ring gauge, uses the least butane (30 to 50 lights a fill), and is the easiest to control on a Corona or a Petit Robusto. The Xikar EX and the Colibri Rally are single jets.

**Double jet:** two flames side by side. Faster on 56-plus ring gauges and steadier in wind, at roughly twice the fuel. The Xikar ELX.

**Triple jet:** three flames, the most heat. Lights a Gordo or a Behike 56 quickly and keeps going in a breeze, but over-toasts thin cigars unless you hold it further away. The Xikar Trezo is an inline triple: the three jets sit in a row, which keeps the flame narrow enough to aim.

If you smoke mostly Cuban Robustos and Coronas indoors, a single jet is all you need. If you smoke big ring gauges outdoors, go double or triple.

## Questions people ask

**What is the best cigar lighter?** For most smokers, a single-jet torch from a maker with a real warranty. Our pick is the Xikar EX. Go double-jet (Xikar ELX) if you smoke outdoors or above 56 ring gauge.

**Is a torch lighter better than matches or a soft flame?** A torch is faster and works in wind, which is why most smokers use one. Cedar spills and long matches give a gentler, slower light that some prefer for an expensive cigar. A soft-flame lighter sits in between.

**How long does a fill of butane last?** On a single jet, roughly 30 to 50 lights. A double or triple jet uses about twice as much.

**Can I fly with a torch lighter?** In the United States, no: the TSA does not allow torch lighters in carry-on or checked bags ([tsa.gov](https://www.tsa.gov/travel/security-screening/whatcanibring/items/torch-lighters)). Rules elsewhere differ by airline and country, so check before you pack, and expect to buy one at your destination.

## Maintenance — the boring part that matters

A clean torch lighter lasts a decade. A dirty one fails in 18 months. Three rules:

- **Bleed the fuel chamber before refilling.** Press the refill valve with a small screwdriver until air stops hissing out. Air in the chamber is the #1 cause of weak flames and ignition failures.
- **Use clean butane.** Cheap butane has impurities that clog the jet. Spend the extra $4 on a premium butane like Colibri or Xikar's own — it's available on Amazon and lasts a year of refills.
- **Adjust the flame down before storing.** A torch left at max output develops carbon buildup faster. Drop it to mid-flame before pocketing.
