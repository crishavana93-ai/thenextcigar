We tracked Cohiba prices across the four most-active European markets — Switzerland, Sweden, Germany, and the UK — and the spread between the cheapest and dearest retailer can hit 30% on the same SKU. Same cigar, same box size, same Habanos S.A. import code. Different price by €500 or more.

Here's the data, the explanation, and what to do about it if you actually want to buy.

## Why does the same box cost €500 more in Switzerland than Sweden?

Three forces drive the spread.

**Country-specific tobacco excise tax.** Switzerland's tobacco excise is comparatively low — Swiss VAT is 8.1%. Germany's excise on cigars is a percentage-plus-fixed structure that adds significantly to the wholesale cost. Sweden adds a heavy excise stamp duty on top of 25% VAT. The retail prices reflect all of this, but not always in the direction you'd expect — Swedish retail can come in cheaper because Sweden's Habanos Nordic AB distributor structure operates with thinner per-box mark-ups than the LCDH-franchise model Switzerland uses.

**Habanos S.A. global price harmonisation.** In 2024 Habanos S.A. completed a two-year price-harmonisation programme that brought European pricing closer to the Hong Kong / Macau market (which had historically been Cohiba's most expensive market). The harmonisation hit Cohiba and Trinidad hardest. Pre-2023 prices on this exact SKU were €700–€900 across Europe. The current €1,500–€2,150 band reflects the post-harmonisation reality.

**Retailer-specific promotional cycles.** Swiss LCDH retailers like Cigarmust run discount cycles on slow-moving inventory — Cohiba Siglo IV at Cigarmust currently sits at CHF 1,450, down from a sticker price of CHF 1,610 (a 10% discount). Germany's Noblego runs less aggressive promotion but maintains broader stock depth. Sweden's retailers cycle in and out of stock more frequently than any other market we track — Cigarrspecialisten was out of Robustos for 11 of the past 12 weeks.

When there's no slack in the supply chain, there's no slack in the pricing.

Three takeaways jump out:

The **Behike spread is the wedge SKU**. A 99% price gap on the same cigar is the single most extreme arbitrage opportunity in the European Cuban market right now. Allocation tightness explains part of it, but the practical impact for the buyer is huge.

**Germany leads on the Siglo IV.** If you smoke Siglo IV regularly, Noblego is your retailer. The €175 box-level savings versus Switzerland adds up over a year of purchases.

**Sweden is the surprise.** Stockholm's retailers (Cigarrummet, Cigarrhyllan) are quietly the cheapest in-stock European Cohiba sources in May 2026 — when you can catch them in stock. The stock-availability problem is real: Swedish retailers receive smaller allocations than German or Swiss ones, so they sell through faster.

## What about Spain, Italy, France, and the rest?

Spain shows up in the Robustos table at €2,012 — but Spain's pricing is essentially the state-regulated PVR (precio de venta al público) set by the Comisionado para el Mercado de Tabacos, and online sale is technically prohibited. The Cigar Smoker Club reservation service skirts this through a personal-shopper model.

Italy and the Netherlands have functioning retailers (Sigari e Tabacchi, Hajenius, LCDH Antwerp) with public per-stick pricing, but the spreads on Cohiba SKUs are tighter and less interesting than the SE/DE/CH/UK quadrant. France bans online tobacco sale entirely.

## The buyer's playbook

If you smoke Cohibas regularly and live in Europe, three pieces of advice from this data.

**Set price alerts on the Behike 52 across Swedish retailers.** Cigarrhyllan and Cigarrspecialisten cycle in and out of stock weekly. When they have stock, they're the cheapest place in Europe to buy this SKU by a country mile. The current spread to Switzerland is €1,500 per box — that's the difference between buying one Behike box per year and buying two.

**Default to Noblego for daily-driver Cohibas.** Siglo IV, Siglo VI, Línea 1492 generally. Germany's price stability, in-stock reliability, and EU shipping make Noblego the practical answer for the working Cuban aficionado who doesn't want to chase allocations.

**Use Switzerland for limited editions and aged stock.** Cigarmust and Siglomundo carry significantly deeper aged-Cohiba inventories than German or Swedish retailers. Pay the premium when the SKU is rare or vintage. Skip the premium when it's a current-production flagship that any allocated retailer can supply.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is Cohiba so expensive in Europe in 2026?

Habanos S.A. completed a two-year global price-harmonisation programme in 2024 that brought European retail pricing closer to the Hong Kong / Macau market. Pre-2023 prices on flagship Cohibas were 40–60% lower than today's prices. The new pricing reflects Habanos's strategic decision to position Cohiba as the global luxury benchmark, not the historical Cohiba price band.

### Which European country has the cheapest Cohiba prices?

For currently-in-stock Cohiba Robustos, **Sweden** (Cigarrummet at SEK 19,200 / €1,670). For Cohiba Behike 52, Sweden (Cigarrhyllan, when in stock) is the cheapest by a wide margin. For Cohiba Siglo IV, **Germany** (Noblego at €1,350) consistently leads. Switzerland is the most expensive on most flagship SKUs but carries the deepest aged and limited-edition stock.

### Is Switzerland always more expensive than the EU on Cohiba?

In absolute terms, generally yes. Switzerland's LCDH retailers carry a premium of 10–30% versus German and Swedish equivalents on current-production Cohibas. The exception is when Swiss retailers run discount cycles (Cigarmust runs frequent 10% drops on Línea 1492 SKUs). Switzerland's value proposition is depth of aged stock, not headline pricing.

### Can I buy Cohiba from a Swiss retailer if I live in the EU?

Yes — Switzerland sits outside the EU customs union, so Swiss retailers can ship into EU countries with customs declaration. Cigarmust, Siglomundo, and CigarOne all ship worldwide. EU buyers may face customs handling on shipments above personal-use quantities (typically more than 50 cigars per shipment).
