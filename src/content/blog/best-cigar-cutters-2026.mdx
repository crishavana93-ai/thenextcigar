## How to actually choose

New to cigars? Start with one double guillotine: the Xikar Xi2 if you want a warranty, the Zertone if you want to spend under $20. Either handles most cigars up to about a 60 ring gauge.

If you smoke a lot of large ring gauges (Toros, Gordos, Magnums), add the Colibri V-Cut. A guillotine and a V-cutter together cover almost any cigar.

If you host other smokers regularly, the KEJIAR tabletop earns its place on the humidor. Nobody wants to ask "can I borrow your cutter?" — and a tabletop with multiple cut options makes that question moot.

The Xikar XO is the upgrade: the same kind of double guillotine with a larger 64 ring-gauge capacity.

## V-cut, straight cut or punch?

**Straight cut (guillotine):** takes the cap off cleanly. The right cut for thin and mid ring gauges, Coronas to Robustos, and for any cigar with a pointed head, like a Montecristo No. 2.

**V-cut:** a wedge-shaped notch that leaves most of the cap on, so the wrapper is less likely to unravel. Best on 50-plus ring gauges with a rounded head.

**Punch:** a round hole in the cap. Neat and quick on a Robusto, but too small an opening for a big ring gauge, and useless on a pointed head.

## Questions people ask

**What is the best cigar cutter?** For one cutter that does everything, a double guillotine: the Xikar XO, or the Xi2 if budget matters. Add the Colibri V-cut if you smoke a lot of 52-plus ring gauges.

**Is a V-cut better than a straight cut?** Not better, different. A V-cut concentrates the draw and protects the cap on big rounded heads; a straight cut is safer on thin cigars and the only sensible cut on a pointed head.

**How much of the cap should I cut?** Only the cap: about 2 mm, stopping above the line where the cap meets the wrapper. Cut below it and the wrapper starts to unravel.

## Maintenance — the part nobody talks about

A good cigar cutter, treated well, lasts for years. Three rules:

1. **Wipe the blades clean after cutting.** Tobacco residue builds up on the edges. A cotton handkerchief is enough.
2. **Don't drop it.** A hard knock can push double blades out of line, and the warranties don't cover accidental damage.
3. **Know what the warranty covers.** Xikar's limited lifetime warranty covers manufacturing defects, not wear from normal use, and Xikar doesn't advertise a sharpening service. Colibri's warranty runs two years. If a blade fails early, claim it; don't take the cutter apart yourself, which voids both warranties.
