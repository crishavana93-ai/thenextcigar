## Before you buy: three things that matter more than the box

**Size: buy bigger than you think.** Makers quote capacity in small cigars. Fill a box with Robustos or thicker cigars and it holds a good deal less; Case Elegance itself says the Renzo takes about 30 to 35 cigars of 52 ring gauge, but 30 to 50 smaller ones. Many smokers outgrow their first humidor sooner than they expect. A half-full box also holds humidity just as well as a full one.

**Humidity: use a two-way pack, not the sponge in the box.** Most humidors ship with a foam or crystal humidifier that you top up with distilled water. It works, but it needs watching. A two-way humidity pack such as Boveda adds or absorbs moisture to hold one level, usually 69, 65 or 62 per cent. That is the cheapest upgrade in this guide and the one that makes the biggest difference.

**A hygrometer you can trust.** The dial hygrometers sold with cheap humidors can be off by several points. A small digital one costs less than $15. Whatever you use, check it against a known level before you trust it.

## How to set up a new humidor

1. **Season it before the cigars go in.** New Spanish cedar is dry and will pull moisture out of your cigars. Raise the wood's humidity first, either with a seasoning pack made for the job (Boveda sells 84% seasoning packs) or by wiping the inside lightly with distilled water and leaving a damp, not wet, humidifier in the closed box. It usually takes one to two weeks.
2. **Check the hygrometer.** Put it in a sealed bag with a fresh humidity pack for a day and see whether it reads the pack's level. Note the difference and allow for it.
3. **Add your cigars and one humidity pack per 25 cigars of capacity.** Keep the humidor out of direct sun and away from radiators; both make it harder to hold humidity.
4. **Leave it closed.** Every time you open a humidor you let the humid air out. Check it weekly, not daily.
