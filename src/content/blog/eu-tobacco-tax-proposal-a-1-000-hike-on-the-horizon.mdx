*Updated 15 November 2026. This explainer replaces our earlier coverage of the proposal, which contained figures and forecasts we could not source. Every number below links to the document it comes from.*

**In short:** The EU sets a minimum excise duty on cigars that every member state must charge. Today it is **€12 per 1,000 cigars, or 5% of the retail price**. In July 2025 the European Commission proposed **€143 per 1,000, or 40%**, almost twelve times the fixed amount. In November 2026 the Council's compromise text cut that to **€80 per 1,000, or 30%, phased in by 2032**. Tax rules need every member state to agree, and on 9 November 2026 Malta withheld its consent. The next attempt is scheduled for 11 December. Latest news: [Malta blocks the deal](/blog/eu-cigar-tax-malta-blocks-deal-november-2026/).

## What the directive does

Directive [2011/64/EU](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2011/64/oj/eng) sets the structure and minimum rates of excise duty on tobacco across the EU. Countries can tax more, and many do. None can tax less. Article 14 sets the floor for cigars and cigarillos at "5 % of the retail selling price inclusive of all taxes or EUR 12 per 1 000 items or per kilogram". A country meets the floor by satisfying either figure.

## The three sets of numbers

| Minimum for cigars and cigarillos | Percentage | Fixed amount per 1,000 cigars or per kg |
|---|---|---|
| Today (Directive 2011/64, Art. 14) | 5% | €12 |
| Commission proposal, 16 July 2025 | 40% | €143 |
| Council compromise, 6 November 2026 (from 2032) | 30% | €80 |

The Commission's figures come from its proposal COM(2025) 580, as set out in the [Swedish government's fact memo](https://data.riksdagen.se/dokument/HD06FPM1.html) of 9 September 2025. The UK government's [explanatory memorandum](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/68c27b51eeb238b20672a971/XST_signed_EM_for_COM_580_and_581__1_.pdf) confirms the publication date and that the proposal "will also need to be agreed unanimously by the Council of the EU".

The compromise figures come from Council document [ST 15388/26](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-15388-2026-INIT/en/pdf), the presidency compromise text of 6 November 2026. Its Article 20 phases the cigar minimum in:

- **1 July 2028 to end of 2029:** 5% or €12 per 1,000, the same as today.
- **2030 and 2031:** 20% or €50 per 1,000.
- **From 2032:** 30% or €80 per 1,000.

The compromise also lets member states that tax cigars by value, or by a mix of value and quantity, set a different minimum amount for cigars and cigarillos.

The fixed amount would rise almost twelve-fold under the Commission's text (143 ÷ 12) and more than six-fold under the compromise (80 ÷ 12). For a country that already taxes above both figures, nothing changes.

## Where it stands

- **9 November 2026:** EU finance ministers met in Brussels. According to [Brussels Signal](https://brusselssignal.eu/2026/11/eu-tobacco-overhaul-stalls-as-commission-watchdog-rejects-impact-assessment/) and [Tobacco Reporter](https://tobaccoreporter.com/2026/11/10/malta-blocks-eu-tobacco-tax-overhaul-in-brussels/), 26 member states backed the compromise and Malta withheld its consent. Tax rules need unanimity, so the reform stalled.
- **11 December 2026:** the next attempt, according to Brussels Signal.
- **If agreed:** it is a directive, so each country then writes the minimums into national law. The compromise text applies the new structure from 1 July 2028.

## What it means in Sweden

Sweden already taxes cigars above both figures. Skatteverket's [current rates](https://www.skatteverket.se/foretag/skatterochavdrag/punktskatter/tobaksskatt/skattesatser.4.46ae6b26141980f1e2d4664.html) put the tax at **1.83 kronor per cigar in 2026**. The government's fact memo converted the Commission's €143 to about **1.70 kronor** per cigar and said the proposal would not require Sweden to raise it. The compromise's €80 is lower still. The countries that would feel a new floor are the ones taxing cigars at or near today's EU minimum.

## What we don't know

- Whether the 11 December meeting will reach unanimity, or whether the cigar figures change again before then.
- How each member state would set its national rates within the new minimums.
- What any of this means for retail prices. Excise is one part of a price, and we don't forecast prices.
