**In short:** The EU sets a minimum tax on cigars that every member state must charge. Today it is **€12 per 1,000 cigars, or 5% of the retail price**. In July 2025 the European Commission proposed raising it to **€143 per 1,000, or 40%**, almost twelve times higher. On **9 November 2026**, EU finance ministers tried to agree the revised tobacco tax law. **26 countries backed a compromise and Malta withheld its consent.** Tax law needs every country to agree, so it stalled. The next attempt is set for **11 December**.

## The numbers

| | Minimum for cigars and cigarillos |
|---|---|
| Today (Directive 2011/64, Article 14) | 5% of the retail price, or €12 per 1,000 cigars or per kilogram |
| Commission proposal, 16 July 2025 | 40% of the price, or €143 per 1,000 cigars or per kilogram |
| Change in the fixed amount | About twelve times higher |

The current rule comes from Article 14 of the tobacco excise directive, [2011/64/EU](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2011/64/oj/eng). The proposed figures are from the Commission's proposal COM(2025) 580, as set out in the [Swedish government's fact memo](https://data.riksdagen.se/dokument/HD06FPM1.html) of 9 September 2025. The UK government's [explanatory memorandum](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/68c27b51eeb238b20672a971/XST_signed_EM_for_COM_580_and_581__1_.pdf) confirms the proposals were published on 16 July 2025 and "will also need to be agreed unanimously by the Council of the EU".

## What happened on 9 November

At the meeting of EU finance ministers in Brussels, the Irish Council presidency put forward a compromise text. According to [Brussels Signal](https://brusselssignal.eu/2026/11/eu-tobacco-overhaul-stalls-as-commission-watchdog-rejects-impact-assessment/), 26 member states backed it and Malta withheld its consent, arguing that taxation remains a national matter. [Tobacco Reporter](https://tobaccoreporter.com/2026/11/10/malta-blocks-eu-tobacco-tax-overhaul-in-brussels/) reports that Malta said the proposed minimum rates did not take enough account of differences in national price levels.

**What we don't know:** the reports on the compromise describe the cigarette rates and the newer nicotine products, but say nothing about cigars. We have not seen the compromise text, so we can't say whether it still contains the €143 figure for cigars.

## What happens next

- **11 December 2026:** a further attempt by EU finance ministers, according to Brussels Signal.
- **The other tobacco laws are delayed too.** On 9 November, the Commission's Regulatory Scrutiny Board gave a second negative opinion on the impact assessment for the planned revisions of the Tobacco Products Directive and the Tobacco Advertising Directive. The Commission says it can no longer present those proposals before the end of 2026 ([Brussels Signal](https://brusselssignal.eu/2026/11/eu-tobacco-overhaul-stalls-as-commission-watchdog-rejects-impact-assessment/), [Tobacco Reporter](https://tobaccoreporter.com/2026/11/13/eu-commission-watchdog-rejects-tobacco-impact-assessment/)).

## What it means in Sweden

Sweden already taxes cigars above the proposed floor. The government's fact memo puts Sweden's tax at **1.82 kronor per cigar** (2025) and converts the proposed EU minimum to about **1.70 kronor**, so the proposal would not require Sweden to raise it. The countries that would feel a new floor are the ones that tax cigars at or near today's EU minimum.
