Short answer: when an accessory ships from outside your customs area, the price on the listing is not the price. Import VAT is due on almost everything, customs duty on some of it, and the carrier charges a fee for collecting both. "Duties paid" means the seller has settled all three before the parcel leaves, so the figure at checkout is the figure, and nobody rings your doorbell with a card reader. Every item in our shop ships that way to Sweden, Germany and the United Kingdom. Here is what you are avoiding.

## The three charges

The first is import VAT, and it is not optional. Since July 2021 the European Union charges VAT on every commercial parcel from outside the Union, whatever its value; the old exemption for small packages is gone. Sweden charges 25 per cent, Germany 19. The United Kingdom, outside the Union since 2021, charges 20 per cent and for low-value goods expects the seller to collect it at the point of sale, which many marketplace sellers do not.

The second is customs duty. Whether it applies depends on the value of the parcel and the tariff line of what is inside, and the thresholds and rates have been changing, in the EU especially, as Brussels tightens the rules on cheap direct-from-China parcels. Lighters and cutters carry duty rates in the low single digits, so on a small parcel the duty itself is rarely the painful part.

The painful part is the third charge: the carrier's handling fee. PostNord in Sweden, DHL or Deutsche Post in Germany, Royal Mail or Parcelforce in the UK all charge a flat fee for presenting the parcel to customs and collecting the tax, and on a small parcel that fee is often larger than the tax it is collecting. It is the reason a marketplace cutter that looked cheap arrives with a slip in the letterbox asking for a payment before delivery, and the reason the total is nowhere near the listing.

## A worked example

Take a lighter listed at US$29 on a marketplace with "free shipping" from a seller in Guangzhou, delivered to Malmö. Import VAT at 25 per cent on the goods and the (not actually free) shipping adds roughly a fifth again. Duty, if it applies, a few per cent more. Then PostNord's handling fee, which on a parcel of this size is the largest single addition. The lighter does not cost $29. It costs $29 plus a surprise, paid to a courier, ten days after you thought you had bought it, and if you decline the surprise the parcel goes back.

The same lighter at $49 from us, duties paid, costs $49. Delivery is tracked post, one to two weeks, and the parcel comes through the door without a slip because there is nothing left to pay. The two prices are closer than the listings suggest, and one of them is honest.

## What "duties paid" actually commits us to

The trade term is DDP, delivered duty paid. The seller is the importer of record, pays VAT and any duty in the destination country, and pays the carrier's fee. The buyer pays the checkout price and nothing else. For a shop like ours, selling accessories that are made in China and shipped from the maker, it is the only arrangement under which we can print a price and mean it.

It also changes who is responsible when something goes wrong. If a DDP parcel is held at customs, that is our problem to solve, not yours; you never deal with a customs office. If it is lost, it is our loss and we send another. The tracking number arrives within 48 hours of the order, and the same number works on the postal service's own site in your country.

## Why not Amazon, then

Amazon's European sites do handle import charges properly for items they sell and ship themselves. The trouble is that a search for a cigar cutter on Amazon.de or Amazon.se returns a page of listings from third-party sellers, most of them shipping direct from China, and whether the price shown includes the charges at the door depends on the seller, the fulfilment route and the day. We priced our shop against Amazon in September 2026 and found the marketplace prices were lower on the listing and, once VAT, duty and the carrier's fee were added, much less lower at the door. The difference is what we charge for being sure.

## Sweden, Germany and the UK in one line each

**Sweden.** 25 per cent VAT on the goods and the postage. PostNord collects, charges its handling fee, and holds the parcel until you pay through its app or site. We pay all of it before the parcel leaves.

**Germany.** 19 per cent VAT. DHL and Deutsche Post collect and charge a fee for the service; parcels can also be routed to a customs office for collection in person if the paperwork is incomplete. We pay all of it before the parcel leaves, and the paperwork is complete because we filled it in.

**United Kingdom.** 20 per cent VAT, collected at the point of sale on low-value goods when the seller is registered for it, otherwise at the door by Royal Mail with a handling fee. We pay all of it before the parcel leaves.

## What is not covered

Duties paid covers the parcel we send. It does not cover the return leg if you change your mind: you pay the return postage, we refund the goods, within 14 days of delivery, as set out on the [shipping and returns page](/shipping-and-returns/). It does not cover countries we do not ship to. And it does not cover the gas: every lighter ships empty because butane cannot fly, and the refill can is a few euros at any tobacconist.

## Questions

### Do I pay customs on cigar accessories ordered to Sweden?

If the seller ships from outside the EU without paying duties, yes: 25 per cent VAT, possibly duty, and PostNord's handling fee before delivery. If the seller ships duties paid, as we do, nothing at the door.

### What does DDP or "delivered duty paid" mean?

The seller pays import VAT, any customs duty and the carrier's collection fee in your country before the parcel is delivered. The checkout price is the final price.

### Why did my parcel from China cost more than the listing?

Import VAT is charged on every commercial parcel into the EU and the UK, and the carrier adds a handling fee for collecting it, which on a small parcel is often more than the tax. Neither was in the listing.

### Are cigar lighters shipped with gas?

No. Butane is dangerous goods on aircraft, so every lighter that crosses a border by air ships empty. You fill it from a standard refill can.

### How long does delivery take to Germany or the UK?

Tracked post, one to two weeks from dispatch; the tracking number is sent within 48 hours of the order.
